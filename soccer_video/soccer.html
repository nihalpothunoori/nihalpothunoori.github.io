Soccer Counterfactual Video Generation

Skills: VAE, Diffusion Transformers, DDPM, T5, CFG

Architecture

Broadly: video frames go into a VAE encoder to produce a compressed latent representation. That latent gets noised and denoised by a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) conditioned on a text prompt, and the VAE decoder reconstructs the final frames from the denoised latent. Implemented from scratch based on the Wan paper.

VAE

The VAE compresses a video of shape [1+T, H, W, 3] down to a latent of [1+T/4, H/8, W/8, C]. The encoder and decoder stack spatial-only downsampling blocks (which compress each frame independently) with spatio-temporal blocks that operate across both space and time simultaneously. The temporal compression is what lets the diffusion model work on a much shorter, cheaper sequence rather than every individual frame.

The VAE is trained by maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO):

Quick walkthrough. The left side is log P(X) — the log likelihood of our training data, which is what we actually want to maximize — minus a KL term D[Q(z|X)||P(z|X)]. Since KL divergence is always ≥ 0 by definition, the right-hand side is always a valid lower bound on log P(X). This means we can ignore that left-side KL entirely and just optimize the right-hand side (the ELBO) as a surrogate.

On the right side, there are two terms:

Ez~Q[log P(X|z)] — the reconstruction loss. Given a latent z sampled from the encoder's distribution, how well can the decoder reproduce the original video frames? This is essentially your pixel-level L2 loss.

D[Q(z|X)||P(z)] — KL regularization. This keeps the encoder's learned distribution close to the standard normal prior. Without it, the encoder just memorizes inputs without learning a smooth, generalizable latent space — useless for generation.

Posterior Collapse

The first training run produced this:

Every generated frame was identical. This is posterior collapse. When the KL term dominates early in training, the encoder gets pushed toward outputting the prior (basically all standard normals) before it ever learns to encode anything meaningful. The decoder sees this — the latent z carries zero information about the input — and adapts by learning to ignore z entirely, instead outputting the mode of the training data for every input. The result: a frozen frame no matter what you put in.

VQVAE

The obvious fix seemed to be constraining the latent space to a finite codebook of learned embeddings (VQVAE). Instead of outputting a continuous Gaussian, the encoder commits to the nearest codebook vector. This forces the latent to represent real, discrete scene states — no more degenerate solutions. I did the research and built it out.

Then came the question of how to noise discrete tokens with DDPM. It's doable — discrete diffusion exists — but it's awkward and non-standard. I also considered swapping diffusion out for a U-Net entirely, but that's not scalable and honestly not that interesting to build. So I scrapped VQVAE and went back to the continuous VAE.

Beta Annealing

Instead, I kept the continuous VAE and added a beta schedule to the KL term — linearly annealing β from 0 up to 1 over training. Starting at β=0 effectively removes the KL penalty entirely, letting reconstruction loss dominate and forcing the encoder to actually learn to encode meaningful content before any regularization kicks in. As β increases, the KL term gradually shapes the latent space without ever collapsing it. Both reconstruction and KL loss improved, and the stagnant frames disappeared.

Diffusion Transformer

With a working VAE in hand, the diffusion process operates entirely in the compressed latent space. A clean latent z0 is noised forward to zt according to:

ᾱt is the cumulative noise schedule product. At t=0 the latent is clean; at t=T it's pure Gaussian noise. The model εθ is trained to predict the noise added at each step, given the noised latent zt, the timestep t, and the text conditioning τθ(y). Loss is plain MSE on the noise prediction.

The DiT block handles two conditioning signals — timestep and text:

Timestep: passed through an MLP, then used for Adaptive Layer Norm (AdaLN) — it modulates the scale and shift of each layer norm inside the block. This lets the model adjust its behavior at different noise levels, which matters a lot: the kind of coarse structure vs. fine detail the model should focus on changes significantly across the noise schedule.

Text: encoded with T5, which produces the token-level embeddings that are injected as cross-attention keys and values, so every visual token in the DiT can attend over the full text sequence at each block.

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) helped a lot with output quality. During training, the text condition is randomly dropped some percentage of the time, teaching the model to also generate unconditionally. (Dropped it 10% in my specific instance.) At inference the guided score is a linear extrapolation: εguided = εuncond + w · (εcond − εuncond). Pushing hard in the direction of the conditioned prediction (high guidance weight) cleared up a lot of the muddy, averaged-looking outputs.

Model Training

Used SoccerNetv2 from HuggingFace — 10 full games with action labels. Split into roughly 2,000 five-second training clips. Trained the VAE first to convergence, then froze it and trained the DiT on top.

At each step, the DiT predicts ε̂ given zt, t, and the text prompt. That predicted noise gets subtracted out, and a small stochastic term z ~ N(0, I) is added back to keep the process from collapsing to the mean. Repeat T times. The final z0 goes straight through the VAE decoder, which generates all frames simultaneously — no autoregressive frame-by-frame rollout.
